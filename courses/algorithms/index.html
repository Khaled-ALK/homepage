A Second Course in Algorithms

Independent Study based on Tim Roughgarden's Stanford CS261 (Winter 2016).

Recently I’ve been studying game theory, and in particular I became curious about how linear programming appears in the proof of von Neumann’s minimax theorem. In parallel, I was learning about machine learning, where I ran into the AdaBoost paradigm and also encountered multiplicative weight updates in the context of multi-armed bandit learning. All of these topics felt strangely related.


Another question that caught my attention was the following: In a repeated game, what happens if both players use regret-free (no-regret) learning algorithms? My first intuition was that in zero-sum games this should somehow converge to an equilibrium, while in more general games it might fail to converge or exhibit more complicated behavior.


I still do not fully understand all of these connections, but they are exactly the reasons this course appealed to me: it brings together linear programming, duality, game-theoretic ideas, and multiplicative weights in one coherent framework.

Topics Covered

Part I – Combinatorial Optimization

  • Maximum flow: Ford–Fulkerson, Edmonds–Karp, Dinic’s
  • Minimum (s)-(t) cuts and applications
  • Reductions between max flow, min cut, matching
  • Min-cost bipartite matching & Hungarian algorithm
  • Generalizations: min-cost flow, non-bipartite matching

Part II – Linear Programming & Duality

  • Modeling optimization problems as LPs
  • LP duality: weak/strong duality, complementary slackness
  • Max-flow/min-cut via duality
  • Applications: matching, minimax theorem
  • Overview of LP algorithms: simplex, ellipsoid, interior-point

Part III – Online Algs & Multiplicative Weights

  • Online decision-making and regret minimization
  • Multiplicative weights update method & analysis
  • Applications to zero-sum games & linear classifiers
  • Classic online problems: scheduling, Steiner tree, matching

Part IV – Algorithms for NP-hard problems

  • Approximation algorithms & ratios (Set Cover, TSP, etc.)
  • LP-based approximation & primal-dual methods
  • Randomized algorithms (Markov, Chebyshev, Chernoff bounds)
  • Fixed-parameter tractability & exact algorithms
  • Semidefinite programming & Max-Cut via SDP relaxations
