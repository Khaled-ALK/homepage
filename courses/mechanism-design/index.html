Frontiers in Mechanism Design

Independent Study based on Tim Roughgarden's Stanford CS364B (Winter 2014).

Advanced topics in mechanism design. Possible topics include ascending auctions and other indirect mechanisms; Bayes-Nash equilibrium analysis; the price of anarchy in simple auctions; correlated and interdependent valuations; black-box reductions in algorithmic mechanism design; revenue maximization in multi-parameter settings.


This course perfectly complements my interest in game theory and optimization. It explores how to design the "rules of the game" so that rational participants naturally converge toward a desirable outcome, bridging the gap between theoretical equilibria and algorithmic tractability.

Topics Covered

Part I – Welfare Maximization

  • Ascending implementations & EPIC/DSIC
  • Unit-demand bidders & Walrasian equilibria
  • Crawford-Knoer & Clinching auctions
  • Gross substitutes and submodular valuations

Part II – DSIC Approx Mechanisms

  • MIR and MIDR mechanisms
  • MIDR mechanisms via scaling algorithms
  • MIDR mechanisms via convex rounding

Part III & IV – Weak Concepts & POA

  • Bayes-Nash equilibria and BIC mechanisms
  • Black-box reductions
  • Price of Anarchy in simple auctions
  • Bayes-Nash POA of 1st & 2nd price auctions

Part V – Multi-Parameter Revenue

  • Recap of single-parameter revenue-max
  • Interim allocation rules and Border's theorem
  • Optimal multi-parameter mechanisms
